# From unital supports to all projective protocol matrices

All vector spaces and parity equations in this note are over `F_2`.
Write `G=[G_1;G_0]`, with `q` logical rows and `h` stabiliser rows, and
write its columns as `(lambda_j,x_j)` in `F_2^q x F_2^h`.

We require full row rank, pairwise distinct complete columns and `d_Z>=3`.
No bound on intrinsic logical dimension is assumed in the correspondence.
Repeated complete columns are excluded, but one zero column is permitted.

## 1. Stabiliser syndromes form a set

If distinct columns have `x_i=x_j`, projectivity implies
`lambda_i != lambda_j`. The error supported on these two positions has zero
stabiliser syndrome and nonzero logical action, contradicting `d_Z>=3`.
Similarly `x_j=0` forces `lambda_j=0`, since otherwise a weight-one error
would be undetected. Consequently

```text
X={x_1,...,x_n} is a set, and lambda(0)=0 if 0 belongs to X.
```

Full row rank of `G_0` says that `X` linearly spans `F_2^h`. The overlaps of
stabiliser rows say that the sum over `X` of every positive-degree monomial
of degree at most three is zero.

## 2. An even unital support

Define `Y=X` for even `n`, and `Y=X triangle {0}` for odd `n`. Positive-degree
monomials vanish at zero, so all their moments remain zero. The constant
moment is now also zero because `|Y|` is even. The matrix with columns
`(1,y)`, for `y` in `Y`, therefore generates a unital triorthogonal space.

Toggling zero does not change a linear span. If the affine hull of `Y` is
`y+D`, its linear span is generated by `D` and `y`, so its affine dimension
is either `h` or `h-1`. This accounts for the two ambient-dimension cases
below. Any dependent generator row in the unital representation is removed.

## 3. All pointings and parity cases

A unital-space catalogue is a catalogue of affine supports. An origin must
be chosen before recovering a protocol stabiliser support. For an affinely
spanning support `Y` in `F_2^m`, choose an origin `b` and translate to
`Y_b={y+b:y in Y}`. The possibilities are:

| Case | Support X | h | Protocol length |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| even | `Y_b` | m | `|Y|` |
| remove origin | `Y_b` without zero, for `b in Y` | m | `|Y|-1` |
| add origin | `Y_b` with zero, for `b notin Y` | m | `|Y|+1` |
| affine hyperplane | `{(1,y):y in Y}` | m+1 | `|Y|` |
| affine hyperplane with zero | `{(1,y):y in Y}` with zero | m+1 | `|Y|+1` |

Retain only supports spanning their declared stabiliser dimension. In the
last two cases the origin is outside the affine hull; all choices outside
that hull are equivalent under affine transformations preserving the
embedded support, so one representative suffices.

For the first three cases, origins in the same orbit of the affine
automorphism group of `Y` yield linearly equivalent supports. Indeed, if
`phi(y)=Ay+t` preserves the support and takes `b` to `b'`, then
`phi(y)+b'=A(y+b)`. Conversely a linear equivalence of pointed supports
lifts to an affine equivalence respecting the chosen origins. Thus origin
orbits give the exact refinement, not merely a sampling rule.

If `n<=54`, every required even support has length at most 54: even `n`
uses length `n`, while odd `n` is at most 53 and uses `n-1` or `n+1`.

## 4. Logical labels as a quotient space

For a fixed stabiliser support `X`, let `V_X` consist of functions
`f:X -> F_2` with

```text
sum_x f(x) m(x)=0
```

for every positive-degree monomial `m` of degree at most two. If zero is
in `X`, also impose `f(0)=0`. Let `L_X` consist of the linear functions
`x -> u dot x` restricted to `X`. The stabiliser moments give `L_X <= V_X`.

For each stabiliser coordinate `a` define

```text
B_a(f,g)=sum_x f(x)g(x)x_a.
```

These bilinear forms descend to `V_X/L_X`: replacing either argument by a
linear function adds a degree-one or degree-two equation already imposed
on the other argument. Their diagonal is zero on `V_X`.

Valid `q`-dimensional logical spaces are exactly the subspaces
`U <= V_X/L_X` of dimension `q` that are simultaneously totally isotropic
for all the `B_a`. Membership in `V_X` checks all overlaps involving one
logical row; common isotropy checks those involving two logical rows and
one stabiliser row. These exhaust the mixed overlap conditions.

Choosing representatives `f_1,...,f_q` gives columns
`(f_1(x),...,f_q(x),x)`. Independence modulo `L_X`, together with the span
of `X`, gives full row rank. Distinct stabiliser syndromes give distinct
complete columns and exclude weight-two logical errors, while `f_i(0)=0`
excludes weight-one errors. Thus every such choice has `d_Z>=3`.

Changing representatives in `V_X/L_X` adds stabiliser rows. Changing a
basis of `U` is a logical basis change. The induced logical tensor on `U` is

```text
T(f,g,k)=sum_x f(x)g(x)k(x).
```

It is independent of the chosen representatives on a common isotropic
subspace: adding `l=sum_a l_a x_a` to one argument changes the value by
`sum_a l_a B_a(g,k)=0`. Terms with two or three changed arguments vanish
by the defining equations of `V_X` or by stabiliser moments. No global
trilinear form on the whole quotient is needed for this argument.

## 5. Higher-distance filtration

For a desired lower bound `d_0`, impose on every logical row the extra
linear equations

```text
sum_{x in E} f(x)=0
```

for every nonempty subset `E` of `X` with `|E|<d_0` and `sum_{x in E}x=0`.
These are exactly the conditions excluding lower-weight undetected logical
errors. Linear stabiliser functions satisfy them, so they define a subspace
of the same quotient. The common-isotropy construction within this filtered
space is exact for `d_Z>=d_0`. Exact distance is evaluated separately.

## 6. Completeness and allowed pruning

Starting from all affine unital supports, taking all pointing cases, and
enumerating all common isotropic subspaces produces every matrix in scope.
The forward and reverse constructions above prove both directions. Raw
pairs can repeat a class until support and label automorphisms are factored
out; they cannot omit one.

A matrix need not admit a projective unital completion obtained by adjoining
logical-only columns. Such a completion is a separate sufficient
construction and is not a filter in this classification.

Every reduced enumeration must separately justify its orbit representatives,
parent eligibility tests, distance filtration and excluded branches. A
Pareto-only intermediate list is not a complete list of logical subspaces:
dominated intermediate protocols can have useful descendants with different
outputs. The admissible-parent rule is stated in `canonical_augmentation.md`.

### Intrinsic outputs at exact distance

Only nonzero magic outputs are Pareto keys; the zero tensor is
Clifford-trivial. Let `R=rad(T|U)` and suppose the intrinsic output
dimension `dim(U/R)` is positive. A degenerate logical space cannot give
a new Pareto point, even when distance is an exact key rather than a lower
bound. The choice of complement is important for this statement.

Choose a minimum-weight undetected logical error `v` for the original
matrix. It induces a nonzero functional `ell_v` on `U`, since its
stabiliser syndrome is zero and its logical action is nonzero. There is
a complement `W` to `R` on which `ell_v` is nonzero. Indeed, start with
any complement. If the functional vanishes there, it is nonzero on some
`r in R`; replace one complement basis vector `w` by `w+r`. This remains
a complement and now detects the same functional.

Restrict the logical rows to this `W`, leaving all stabiliser rows
unchanged. The output tensor is the nondegenerate quotient tensor on
`U/R`, because all contractions with `R` vanish. No error of weight below
the original distance becomes logical, while the selected minimum-weight
error remains logical. The exact distance is therefore unchanged.
Projectivity persists because the stabiliser columns were already
distinct, and independence modulo `L_X` gives full row rank. The length
is unchanged and `S` decreases by `dim(R)`.

Consequently it is sufficient to enumerate radical-free logical outputs
when forming this Pareto frontier. An arbitrary fixed complement need not
preserve exact distance; the argument above proves existence of a suitable
one, which the complete nondegenerate logical-subspace enumeration includes.
The leading error coefficient can change, but it is not a Pareto objective.

### Zero-column domination

Although a zero complete column is allowed in the matrix scope, it can
never occur in a Pareto-optimal matrix. Delete that column. All overlaps,
row dependencies and the output tensor are unchanged. A minimum-weight
logical error never uses the deleted position: removing that position
from an error leaves both its stabiliser syndrome and its logical action
unchanged and decreases its weight. Conversely every error on the shorter
matrix is an error on the original matrix. Thus the exact distance and
its leading error coefficient are unchanged, as is `S`, while `n`
decreases by one.

This is also an exact exclusion of both the "add origin" and "affine
hyperplane with zero" cases from a final Pareto frontier, because the
distance-three condition forces every logical label at zero to vanish.
The even case has the same exclusion whenever its pointing origin lies
in the unital support. The complete five-case census remains a valid
way to reproduce the recorded finite counts; only three case types can
contribute to the final frontier.

In particular, protocols at odd length `n` obtained from a parent of
length `n-1` are dominated by shorter protocols in the same scope. Only
parents of length `n+1`, with one point removed, can contribute a new
Pareto point at that odd length. This statement fixes exact distance and
does not rely on replacing it with a distance lower bound.

## 7. Kernel interfaces

`native_core.hpp` constructs `V_X/L_X` and the forms `B_a`.
`origin_orbits.hpp` treats the pointed cases.
`marked_code_canonical.hpp` enumerates logical subspaces under coordinate
automorphisms. `protocol_checks.py` independently verifies complete matrices,
including exact distance and the intrinsic output.
